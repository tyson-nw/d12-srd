The [[Game Master]] is the architect of the world, its people, and its challenges. They are not the creator of the game's story. That is generated as the [[Player]]s direct their [[Character]]s through the [[Set Piece]]s the [[Game Master]] presents.

# [[Agency]]

[[Player]] come to the game to solve problems and make discoveries through the actions of their [[Character]]s. Their [[Character]]s are their sole means of having agency in the world, as mediated by the D12. Taking away that agency runs against the goals of the game to create an interesting story.

It is on the [[Game Master]] to provide enough information that they can make informed decisions based on the knowledge their [[Character]] would have. The better the world is described the more information the [[Player]]s have to make choices with.

# [[Set Piece]]s

The [[Player]]s only experience the game through the [[Game Master]]'s description of the current Set Piece. They should highlight details that allow the [[Player]]s to make informed decisions about their [[Character]]'s actions. By picking and choosing what is described, the [[Game Master]] can set the mood, express themes, and encourage [[Player]]s to interact with [[Scene Feature]]s.  

## [[Environment]]

The characters are always in a place. That place has traits and contents that inform the [[Player]]'s choices, either by presenting obstacles or offering advantages.  Environmental storytelling can reinforce the themes of the [[Conflict]], providing a maximum amount of information with a minimum amount of words.

### [[Light]]

Each light source casts an [[Aura]] of [[Bright Light]] of a specific radius and an aura of Low Light twice the radius.

- **[[Daylight]]** Daylight is natural light from the sun. Its [[Dim Light]] is still considered Daylight for [[Feature]]s where it is relevant.
- **[[Bright Light]] #** Bright light is easy to see within the listed [[Space]]s and confers no cover to creatures within it.
- **[[Dim Light]]** Dim Light is difficult to see in. Targets in Dim Light are [[Screened]]. Dim Light continues beyond the [[Bright Light]] by the listed [[Space]]s.
- **[[Darkness]]** [[Creature]]s in darkness are in [[Cover]] and [[Blinded]] unless they have special vision.

### [[Visibility]]

Who can see what is an important aspect of a [[Conflict]] and can add [[Complication]]s for the [[Character]]s to overcome or opportunities to take advantage of. It is assumed that all creatures can see within areas of Bright Light without [[Hindrance]]. In Low Light [[Senses]] [[Roll]]s have a [[Hindrance]] to see things within the area and [[Stealth]] [[Roll]]s within the area have an [[Edge]]. There are other forms of vision that Creatures may have access to that negate or limit the need for light.

#### Special Vision

Some [[Ancestry|Ancestries]] and [[Feature]]s provide Special Vision.

- **[[Dim Light Vision]]** Areas of [[Dim Light]] are considered [[Bright Light]] and [[Senses]] [[Roll]]s don't have a [[Hindrance]] to see things within them.
- **[[Blindsight]] #** [[Invisible|Invisibility]] and [[Light]] are ignored within a set range.
- **[[Tremorsense]] #** [[Invisible|Invisibility]] of creatures touching the ground and [[Light]] are ignored within the listed [[Space]]s.

#### [[Cover]]

If a [[Creature]] is fully obscured from the vision of others, either behind something solid, within darkness, or other effect. Observers who were aware of the [[Creature]]'s position before they moved to Cover still are aware of them, unless they become [[Hidden]].

#### [[Hidden]]

A [[Character]] becomes Hidden if they can make an [[Antagonist]] unaware of them. Once behind cover, as an [[Action]] they can make a [[Wits]] + [[Stealth]] [[Roll]] against their observer's [[Mind]] and move up to half their [[Stride]] and their observer will not notice the [[Character]] changed position. The [[Character]] will remain Hidden until they draw attention to themself by using a [[Feature]] that deals Damage or makes noise. [[Creature]]s unaware of the Hidden [[Character]] are [[Exposed]] to that [[Creature]].

#### [[Invisible]]

A creature is Invisible if they cannot be seen, either by an [[Enigma]], a [[Feature]], or they are in complete darkness. A [[Character]] can estimate the [[Space]] of the Invisible [[Creature]] with a [[Wits]] + [[Senses]] [[Roll]] against the [[Creature]]'s [[React]]. If the [[Antagonist]] is moving stealthily at half their [[Stride]], the [[Roll]] has a [[Hindrance]]. If the [[Character]] is invisible, as a Maneuver they can move half their Stride to make a [[Dexterity]] + [[Stealth]] [[Roll]] against observers' [[React]] with an [[Edge]].

#### [[Screened]]

If a [[Character]] has at least half of their behind something solid and opaque they are considered Screened and gain an edge on [[React]] and [[Deflect]] from [[Ranged]], [[Thrown]] or area [[Feature]]s.

## [[Scene Feature]]s

Scene Features are aspects of the scene that a [[Character]] or [[Antagonist]] can use to perform a [[Feature]]. Typically [[Scene Feature]]s can be used with an [[Interaction]] or [[Maneuver]] to manipulate the source of the [[Feature]]. For [[Feature]]s with drastic consequences they may require multiple [[Action]]s performed by multiple [[Character]]s or [[Antagonist]]s to activate.

## [[Terrain Feature]]

Terrain Features are passive [[Feature]]s that the [[Character]]s and [[Antagonist]]s interact with within the [[Set Piece]].

### [[Aquatic Terrain]]

When an area of water the [[Character]]s need to swim through, unless a creature has a [[Swim]] [[Feature]] they move at half their [[Stride]] and need to make a [[Strength]] + [[Athletics]] roll with a against the amount of [[Slot]]s they have filled every round or begin to sink uncontrollably 2 [[Space]]s at the end of each [[Round]].

#### [[Holding Breath]]

A [[Creature]] can make a Maneuver before they submerge to hold their breath by making a [[Fortitude]] + [[Focus]] [[Roll]], they can hold it for as many rounds as they [[Roll]], [[Action]]s expend 2 additional [[Round]]s of held breath and [[Maneuver]]s expend 1 additional [[Round]]. If a [[Creature]] takes damage while holding their breath a [[Fortitude]] + [[Focus]] [[Roll]] against the damage taken is required. If the creature fails or runs out of breath they begin [[Suffocating]].

### [[Difficult Terrain]]

Moving through one [[Space]] of Difficult Terrain costs twice the [[Space]]s of the [[Creature]]'s movement.

### [[Falling]] Hazard

Every [[Round]] a creature can fall up to 20 [[Space]]s. For each [[Space]] the [[Creature]] falls before hitting the a solid object they take 1d6 of [[Damage]].

#### [[Catch an Ally]]

[[Character]]s adjacent to a [[Falling]] [[Creature]] can attempt to use a [[Response]] to catch them with a [[Strength]] + [[Athletics]] [[Proactive Roll]] against a [[Target Number]] of 6 + the [[Space]]s the creature has fallen so far. If the [[Character]] and [[Falling]] [[Creature]] are tied together, the [[Character]] has an [[Edge]] on that roll, but on a [[Failure]] the [[Character]] also begins [[Falling]].

### [[Hazardous Terrain]]

Hazardous Terrain deals [[Damage]], applies a [[Condition]], or other effect when a [[Creature]] begins their turn within the terrain or they enter the terrain. Steam vents, vicious brambles, or spinning blades are all Hazardous Terrain. The Hazardous Terrain may have a [[Target Number]] to avoid the damage with a [[React]] [[Defense]] when the [[Character]] is not aware of the hazard or a [[Character]] can use an [[Action]] to make a [[Dexterity]] + [[Athletics]] [[Conflict Roll]] to move through and avoid the hazards.

#### [[Trap]]s

Traps are Terrain Features that have a [[Response]] to a [[Trigger]]. Like all [[Response]]s the [[Trigger]]  could be moving into a specific [[Space]], performing a type of [[Action]], or getting a specific result on a [[Roll]].

> ###### [[Examining an Environment]]
>
> As a Prolonged Action the characters can carefully search a moderately sized room with 3 [[Scene Feature]]s in 10 minutes. Each Up to three [[Character]]s make an [[Aptitude]]+[[Senses]] [[Roll]] each against the highest TN of the [[Scene Feature]]s. The remaining Characters can assist if they are Proficient in [[Senses]] or have an applicable [[Expertise]]. On 3 [[Success]]es the Characters find all 3 Scene Features. On 2 Successes they find 2. On 1 Success they find 1 and trigger 1 hostile feature or cause some other [[Complication]]. With 0 Successes they discover no features and all hostile features are triggered.

# Determining [[Difficulty]]

Core to presenting the challenges to the [[Player]]s is determining the Difficulty of tasks and adjudicating how succeeding or failing will effect the story, as well as generating the [[Perk]]s and [[Complication]]s their attempts may create.

|     Difficulty     | [[Target Number\|TN]] | Description                                                                                                           |
| :----------------: | :-------------------: | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|   **[[Simple]]**   | 5                     | Anyone with a passing familiarity with the task can achieve it.                                                       |
|    **[[Easy]]**    | 7                     | Anyone trained in the task can successfully achieve it.                                                               |
|  **[[Moderate]]**  | 9                     | This takes some effort to achieve, but can be achieved with time or focus.                                            |
|    **[[Hard]]**    | 12                    | Even with time and focus it requires a steady hand, extensive training, or luck.                                      |
| **[[Improbable]]** | 16                    | Only those with mastery of a task can push themselves this far to hope to achieve this level of outcome.              |
| **[[Impossible]]** | 24                    | This is the realm of gods and immortals. Only the highest [[Tier]] [[Character]]s can hope to succeed at these tasks. |

# Deciding When to [[Roll]]

Rolling should only be done when a [[Character]]'s action can have a meaningful effect on the story. If failing doesn't provide an opportunity for new challenges, or a potential [[Perk]] would not meaningly benefit the [[Character]], the [[Game Master]] should give the [[Player]] the option of [[Success|Succeeding]] without a [[Roll]].

As well, the [[Player]]s' proposed actions or roleplay may remove the need for a [[Roll]]. A clever solution using their items, an eloquent argument, or other inventive solutions where [[Failure]] would grind the story to a halt should be rewarded by not requiring a [[Roll]].

## [[Automatic Success]]es

If the [[Difficulty]] is [[Simple]] and the Character has is [[Proficient]] in the applicable [[Skill]], or has an appropriate [[Expertise]] they should automatically [[Success|Succeed]]. If the [[Difficulty]] is [[Easy]] and the character both is [[Proficient]] in the applicable [[Skill]] and has an appropriate [[Expertise]] they should automatically [[Success|Succeed]].

# Player Proposals

[[Player]] will often offer to roll to achieve a task. Before they [[Roll]] the [[Game Master]] should ask what they are trying to achieve. The [[Game Master]] needs to know what the desired result is before they can determine the [[Difficulty]] of the action, what [[Stat]] and [[Skill]] will be appropriate, if an [[Expertise]] applies, and what the [[Complication]] and [[Perk]] will be before the [[Roll]]. Also, the [[Player]] may have one idea of what they can achieve that contradicts what the [[Game Master]] thinks. It is best to resolve that ambiguity before the [[Roll]] is made.

> ###### [[Expertise]]
> 
> Be aware of the [[Character]]’s [[Expertise]]s and allow them to apply the +2 bonus to any [[Roll]] they may be appropriate for, even if not noted in the [[Roll]]'s description.

# Creating [[Perk]]s and [[Complication]]s

[[Perk]]s and [[Complication]]s should be thematically tied to what the [[Roll]] is trying to achieve, but does not have to be causally linked. Regardless of which is [[Roll]]ed, they should both push the story forward.

A [[Complication]] should add a new challenge for the [[Character]]s to overcome but not negate their [[Success]]. If they are sneaking they shouldn't be detected, but they may encounter [[Difficult Terrain]], a reward if they take an additional risk, or increase the consequences of a subsequent [[Failure]]. 

[[Perk]]s should always be a reward for risking [[Failure]]. It can be [[Treasure]], a clue, an [[Edge]] on a subsequent [[Roll]], or other reward that pushes the story forward.

# [[Mount]]s, [[Pet]]s, and [[Vehicle]]s

There are times when the characters command other [[Creature]]s or [[Vehicle]]s. The are differentiated by if they can think for themselves ([[Mount]]s and [[Pet]]s) and those who only respond to commands ([[Vehicle]]s). This means things like animal draw carriages count as [[Mount]]s not [[Vehicle]]s as they can think and act on their own. [[Mount]]s, [[Pet]]s, and [[Vehicle]]s act on a [[Creature]]'s turn, interrupting it when the [[Creature]]'s commands them until their commanded actions are resolved.

## [[Mount]]s

Commanding a [[Mount]] is a [[Will]] + [[Pilot]] [[Proactive Roll]] against a [[Target Number]] depending on the [[Mind]] of the animal. If multiple animals are working in concert the [[Roll]] is only made once and applied to all [[Mount]]s. All [[Mount]]s can be commanded with the [[Character]]'s [[Maneuver]] to move as a [[Maneuver]], move as an [[Action]], or [[Dodge]] as an [[Action]]. As a [[Complication]] the [[Mount]] will complete the action but attempt to throw the rider requiring a [[React]] [[Defense]] against the [[Mount]]'s [[Body]]. Failing to command an untrained animal results in the animal fleeing or attacking their rider if they have an [[Quarrelsome]] or [[Hostile]] [[Demeanor]]. 

> ###### [[Battle Trained]] Mounts
> 
> Commanding a mount to move, [[Dodge]], or attack do not require [Proactive Rolls], but do require a Maneuver from the [[Character]] that includes movement.

## [[Pet]]s

[[Pet]]s have a single [[Character]] that they consider an [[Allied|Ally]] and will accept commands from. Commanding a pet requires a [[Maneuver]] and they take their turn as soon as they are commanded, interrupting their [[Allied|Ally]]'s turn. Commanding a [[Pet]] requires a [[Will]] + [[Social]] [[Proactive Roll]] against the as a [[Maneuver]] to command them to move as an [[Action]], [[Hide]] as an [[Action]], [[Dodge]] as an [[Action]], and move as a [[Maneuver]]. Otherwise the [[Pet]] will avoid threats.

> ###### Commanding [[Battle Trained]] [[Pet]]s
> 
> Commanding [[Battle Trained]] [[Pet]]s requires a [[Maneuver]] that includes movement and doesn't require a [[Roll]]. The pet interrupts their [[Allied|Ally]]'s turn until they complete their [[Turn]], then the master completes their [[Turn]]. If an [[Allied|Ally]] doesn't command a [[Battle Trained]] [[Pet]], they will repeat the commands from the previous turn.

## [[Vehicle]]s

Vehicles, have the seven [[Stat]]s: [[Strength]], [[Fortitude]], [[Dexterity]], [[Deflect]], [[Durability]], [[Soak]], and [[Maximum HP]], but no mental [[Stat]]s so cannot act on their own (unless they are sentient) and as a [[Maneuver]] can be commanded from the pilot's position. If the vehicle is moving it requires a [[Hold Steady]] [[Maneuver]] to maintain speed and direction. Driving a vehicle as a [[Maneuver]] requires a [[Wits]] + [[Pilot]] [[Proactive Roll]] with the[[ Target Number]] set by the terrain and its hazards.

> ###### [[Emplaced]] [[Weapon]]s
> 
> [[Weapon]]s built into a vehicle have the [[Emplaced]] [[Tag]] and require an [[Action]] to operate. If the weapon requires aiming with a vehicle make a [[Wits]] + [[Pilot]] [[Conflict Roll]] to use it. If the [[Emplaced]] [[Weapon]] can be aimed independently of the vehicle requires a [[Wits]] + [[Weapons]] [[Conflict Roll]] to use it.

### Pilots and [[Passenger]]s

If the pilot and [[Passenger]]s of a vehicle go on the same turn, the pilot always acts first, declaring and resolving tasks before the [[Passenger]]s can take their [[Turn]].
