Adventures are a string of [[Conflict]]s where each leads into the next. Though Adventures are stand alone plots and create their own stories, they can include hooks for the next Adventure or be one subplot in a larger Campaign. Each Adventure should explore at least one theme that ties the plot together. It should be made up of a variety of conflicts that challenge the [[Player]]s in different ways.

# [[Hook]]s

Typically Adventures have a Hook that gets the [[Player]]'s interest and introduces the scope of the Adventure, the [[Conflict]]s, and potential Resolutions creating a self contained story.  Hooks can range from rumors that tempt the [[Player]]s with enticing [[Rewards]], to Patrons with a need for the [[Character]]s to fulfil, to [[Antagonist]]s that have a goal running counter to the [[Character]]s', to an immediate emergency involving the [[Character]]s.

# Preparation

Once the [[Player]]s have decided to follow a hook they should prepare to follow it. This should include gathering information on the [[Adventure]], buying supplies, and otherwise preparing to travel to the [[Adventure]]. Any of these could be [[Conflict]]s at the [[Game Master]]'s discretion or if the [[Player]]s want to expand them beyond just rolling. 

[[Game Master]]s can control the urgency of the adventure by setting how long the [[Character]]'s have to prepare. They may grant a [[Downtime]] to prepare for a large social event, infiltration, or expedition into the unknown. They may grant a day to prepare for a journey to as little as hours. Or there may be no Preparation as the Adventure my happen to them.

## Shopping

The characters should be able to find Items without rolling depending on the [[Settlement]] size and the item's [[Base Cost Multiplier]] that costs less than the Max Coin for the [[Settlement]]. 

|   Settlement   | Multiplier | Max Coin  |
| :------------- | ---------- | --------- |
|  **Village**   | X2         | 1,000     |
|    **Town**    | X3         | 10,000    |
|    **City**    | X5         | 100,000   |
| **Metropolis** | X7         | 1,000,000 |

Otherwise to find a non-[[Empowered Item]] make an [[Aptitude]] + [[Social]] against a [[Target Number]] of 6 + Item's [[Base Cost Multiplier]] (which cannot be higher than a Settlement can support) to find a seller. If they want an [[Empowered Item]] they will need to use the Find an Empowered Item to Purchase [[Downtime]] Activity.

## Gathering Information

With enough forewarning [[Character]]s can Gather Information on the adventure as a Downtime Activity. If they only have hours, they can make the same [[Complex Roll]] receiving the same facts but not [[Edge]]s. On a [[Success]] they find 1 useful fact about the target that will give them an [[Edge]] on a Roll of their choice relating to the target. As a [[Perk]] they learn 3 useful facts that will give them [[Edge]]s on 3 different [[Roll]]s relating to the target. As a [[Complication]] they learn 2 useful facts that only one is true and will give an [[Edge]] on a single [[Roll]] related to the true fact, or the target is alerted that they are being investigated. On a [[Failure]] they learn nothing and the target or a competitor is alerted. 

 > ###### Competitors
 > 
 > Competitors are useful [[Antagonist]]s as they can push the [[Player]]s to race for their goal if someone else is also seeking it. They don't have to have a [[Hostile]] [[Demeanor]] to the [[Character]]'s, but they can't have anything higher than an [[Indifferent]] [[Demeanor]] to be Competitors.


# [[Random Encounter]]s

Random Encounters can break up travel and provide an interesting encounter for the [[Character]]s to overcome. As these are world-building aids, it is appropriate for the [[Game Master]] to roll them, but having the players roll for their own potential doom can be fun. When rolling a Random Encounter, rolling twice on the table and combine the outputs to produce more dynamic encounters with two [[Antagonist]]s that can be in [[Conflict]] with each other and the [[Character]]s than just rolling once for one [[Antagonist]]. Depending on the relative danger or safety of the terrain either adds or subtracts a modifier from the roll, as can the [[Navigation Roll]]. Modifiers cannot lower the roll below 1.

| 1d12  | Encounter Demeanor        |
| :---: | ------------------------- |
|   1   | [[Supportive]] encounter  |
|  2-3  | [[Indifferent]] encounter |
|  4-6  | Empty encounter           |
|  7-9  | [[Quarrelsome]] encounter |
| 10-16 | [[Hostile]] encounter     |

# Point Crawl

Traveling to the adventure should also be a challenge. While traveling through well known or charted territory the party moves between a start and end [[Point]] with one [[Point of Choice]] between them. Typically a [[Point of Choice]] is never more than a day beyond the previous Point. It is advised that the journey is no more than 4 [[Point of Choice|Points of Choice]] a day before they reach a [[Point of Shelter]].

## Preparation

The [[Character]]s should have the opportunity to chart out their potential [[Point]]s and [[Leg]]s to choose from, a [[Gather Information]] [[Roll]] should produce information for all [[Point]]s and [[Leg]]s that the [[Player]]s can choose from and minimal details so they can make somewhat informed decisions.

> ###### Caravans
> 
> If the [[Player]]s don't want to handle the navigation or preparation they can [[Gather Information]] to find a caravan they can join. Caravans can support a maximum lifestyle that a character can pay for to have provisions and shelter provided for them. They pay the [[Lifestyle]] cost up front for every day caravan is planning on taking to reach the Destination Point. Caravans can still have Encounters and be redirected if a Leg seems to dangerous to the caravan leader or if they are convinced by the [[Character]]s to take a different path.
> 
> | [[Lifestyle]] | Cost |
> |:---:|--:|
> | **[[Poor]]** | 7 |
> | **[[Comfortable]]** | 70 |
> | **[[Wealthy]]** | 700 |
> | **[[Noble]]** | 7,000 |

## [[Point]]s

Points are places where the [[Character]]s can find shelter to rest between [[Leg]]s or choose what [[Leg]] they are going to take.

### [[Point of Choice|Points of Choice]]

Points of Choice are those where the [[Player]]s can choose their path. It is advised that every Point Crawl have at least one Point of Choice. The first [[Point]] can be a Point of Choice. Typically Points of Choice don't include [[Conflict]]s.

Characters should be able to glean some information about the different available [[Leg]]s, either before they begin the journey or once they reach the [[Point of Choice]] or both. They may have made a [[Gather Information]] [[Roll]] on all their [[Leg]]s before they set out and may choose their path then. But as they progress, the events of the journey should challenge that plan and force the characters to reconsider their chosen path. The [[Game Master]] shouldn't do this by blocking their chosen path, but make other paths more attractive to the [[Player]]s.

### [[Point of Shelter|Points of Shelter]]

Points of Shelter provides shelter, food, and drink for a [[Comfortable Rest]] at a Cost depending on what [[Lifestyle]] the [[Character]]s want to maintain and that the settlement will support.

| [[Settlement]]                        | [[Poor]] | [[Comfortable]] | [[Wealthy]] | [[Noble]] |
| :------------------------------------ | :------: | --------------: | ----------: | --------: |
| **Empty Building <br>or Campsite** |    0^    |              -- |          -- |        -- |
| **Road House**                        |    3     |              30 |          -- |        -- |
| **Village**                           |    5     |              50 |          -- |        -- |
| **Town**                              |    5     |              50 |         500 |        -- |
| **City**                              |    5     |              50 |         500 |     5,000 |
| **Metropolis**                        |    5     |              50 |         500 |     5,000 |

^Does not provide a Day's Rations.

> ###### [[Lifestyle]] Effects
> 
> [[Lifestyle]] affects how members of different social classes see you. Taking a [[Day's Rest]] with a [[Lifestyle]] quality effects the next day or next [[Downtime]]. Transitory shelter costs more per day than longer term support for a [[Lifestyle]] during [[Downtime]]. 

## [[Leg]]

Between each [[Point]] is a Leg where a [[Set Piece]] can happen. These can be beneficial encounters or an encounter with a conflict. If using [[Random Encounter]]s, the [[Navigation Roll]] of those leading the journey should influence which is encountered. 

### Path Quality

The quality of the path between [[Point]]s determines the Leg's the[[ Target Number]]. If the [[Character]]s are in charge of leading, then they need to make a [[Navigation Roll]] against the [[Target Number]] of the Leg. The more populated and traveled of a road the easier the [[Target Number]] as decided by the [[Game Master]]. But unless they are traveling along a canal or well maintained and patrolled freeway with clear markings of side paths or where they cannot have encounters they will still need to make [[Navigation Roll]]s.

> ###### [[Navigation Roll]]
>
> Navigation between points takes a [[Complex Roll]] of an [[Aptitude]] + [[Knowledge]] to chart the path; [[Wits]] + [[Senses]] to be aware of threats on the road; and [[Fortitude]] + [[Athletics]] to keep a pace that all party members can maintain. The [[Target Number]] should be based on the familiarity and how clearly marked the path is. As with all [[Complex Roll]]s, there should be a [[Perk]] that either speeds up their journey or provides the party with a beneficial encounter or both before they reach the next [[Point]] or get a -4 on the [[Random Encounter]] roll. As a [[Success]] they should reach the next [[Point]] when expected. As a [[Complication]] they have an encounter with a Conflict or a [[Random Encounter ]]with a +2 on the roll. And on a [[Failure]] they should get lost, requiring spending additional time to find their location and make a new [[Navigation Roll]] to get them back on track, possibly with an [[Random Encounter]] with a +4 on the roll.

## Travel Times and Distance

[[Leg]]s can have various distances between [[Point]]s. It is best for the [[Game Master]] to decide how many hours traveling each [[Leg]] under clear conditions. Then adjust the time based on the Weather Time Multiplier. At a Walking pace the [[Character]]s can travel 3 miles (5 km) per hour across level clear terrain or on a well kept road. Weather, terrain, or other hazards may slow the travel increasing the hours it takes to travel 3 miles by a multiplier. 

### Terrain Modifiers

Depending on how steep or difficult the overall terrain is can change how many hours it takes to walk 3 miles (or 5km).

|   Terrain   | Time Modifier |
| :--------- | ------------- |
| Flat Plains | +0 Hours      |
|    Hills    | +1 Hours      |
|  Mountains  | +2 Hours      |
|   River^    | +1 Hour       |

^Only if there are no marked fords or bridges.

### Weather Modifiers

Weather Time Multipliers are applied to the total travel time taking into account of terrain.

|   Weather    | Modifier                                 | Weather Time Multiplier |
| :----------: | ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
|    Clear     | None                                     | X1                      |
| Extreme Heat | Hindrance on Athletics Rolls             | X1                      |
|  Light Rain  | Increase TN of navigation by 2           | X1                      |
|  Heavy Mud^  | Hindrance on Athletics Rolls             | X2                      |
|  Heavy Rain  | Hindrance on Senses Rolls.               | X2                      |
|     Hail     | Take 1d6 Damage per hour of travel.      | X3                      |
|     Snow     | Hindrance on Senses and Athletics Rolls. | X4                      |

^ Does not effect high quality roads.

### Speed Modifiers

These numbers assume a [[Stride]] of 5 or 6. If the [[Character]]s can move faster with [[Mount]]s or [[Vehicle]]s. If they have a different speed, divide the movement by 6 and then multiply that by the total time to get how many hours it takes to travel 3 miles (or 5km).

# Hex Crawl

When exploring new territory with little to no information or searching for a location the Hex Crawl is the best tool for the job. There are two ways to generate the map, either the [[Game Master]] builds the map before the game begins and reveals the hexes as the party moves through them or the map is generated as the characters explore with Hexflowers or other Random Tables.  Each [[Hex]] should either have an encounter predetermined by the [[Game Master]] if they built the map or a [[Random Encounter]] based on the [[Hex]] 's details.

> ###### Hex Size
> 
> Each [[Hex]] should be 3 miles across or 5 km based on the [[Game Master]] preference.

## Preparation

Preparation for a Hex Crawl is key to their survival. [[Character]]s should be granted time to [[Gather Information]] either with a single day, or [[Downtime]]s to [[Gather Information]] on the target and ensure they can plan their expedition. 

## [[Hex]]

Every Hex has a [[Target Number ]]used by the [[Navigation Roll|Navigation]] and [[Survey Roll]]s.

### [[Familiarity]]

As the [[Character]]'s understanding of a [[Hex]] improves their Familiarity increases from [[Unknown]], to [[Explored]], then to [[Charted]]. Every time they gain a [[Perk]] on their [[Navigation Roll]] they increase their Familiarity. 

If the [[Character]]s want to increase their [[Familiarity]] they can make a [[Survey Roll]] and spend the current traversal time exploring the [[Hex]]. Once its Familiarity is increased the [[Character]]s can traverse it with the next [[Navigation Roll]] and spend the new time to traverse to a new [[Hex]].

- **[[Unknown]] [[Hex]]** The party is entering the Hex for the first time. Traversing an Unknown [[Hex]] takes 3 hours and has a [[Target Number]]  of 10.
- **[[Explored]] [[Hex]]** The party has passed through the [[Hex]] previously and is familiar with the terrain. Traversing an Explored [[Hex]] takes 2 hours and has a [[Target Number]] of 6.
- **[[Charted]] [[Hex]]** The party has spent time charting the Hex previously and has a deep understanding of the terrain. Traversing a Charted [[Hex]] takes 1 hour and has a [[Target Number]] of 4. 
- **A Road** Traversing along a road from one Hex to another Hex that road connects grants an Edge on the [[Aptitude]] + [[Knowledge]] [[Roll]] of the [[Navigation Roll]] to traverse the [[Hex]] between the connected [[Hex]]es. It is assumed that Roads in [[Hex]]es are not maintained and cannot be truly relied on. If the Road is well kept and reliable, the Characters can automatically succeed their [[Navigation Roll]].

> ###### [[Survey Roll]]
> 
> To increase the Character's [[Familiarity]] with a Hex they make a [[Complex Roll]] against the [[Target Number]] of the [[Hex]]. As a [[Perk]] gain subtract a -4 to the [[Random Encounter]] rolls and as a [[Complication]] add a +2 to the [[Random Encounter]] rolls and increase the [[Familiarity]]. On a [[Failure]] do not increase the [[Familiarity]].

### Terrain 

The severity of terrain makes navigating and surveying harder requiring more time to Traverse.

|    Terrain    | Target Number | Traversal Time |
| :------------ | ------------- | -------------- |
|   Flatlands   | +0            | +0 Hours       |
|   Low Hills   | +1            | +1 Hours       |
| Rolling Hills | +2            | +2 Hours       |
|    Ridges     | +3            | +3 Hours       |
|   Mountains   | +4            | +4 Hours       |
|    Rivers     | -1            | +2 Hours^      |

^Only while [[Hex]] is [[Unknown]].

### Biome

The biome of the Hex can modify the [[Roll]]s of Characters as they traverse them.

|     Biome      | Modifier                                                                     |
| :------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|     Plains     | No Modifier.                                                                 |
|   Scrubland    | [[Edge]] on [[Stealth]] [[Roll]]s when moving at half pace.                  |
| Lightly wooded | [[Edge]] on [[Stealth]] [[Roll]]s.                                           |
|  Thick woods   | [[Hindrance]] on [[Senses]] [[Roll]]s and [[Edge]] on [[Stealth]] [[Roll]]s. |

### Weather

The weather can effect the character's ability to traverse a Hex, increasing the [[Hex]]'s [[Target Number]] and the time to traverse it.

|   Weather    | Modifier                                                                                                                                    | Time Multiplier |
| :----------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Extreme Heat | Hindrance on Athletics Rolls                                                                                                                | X1              |
|  Light Rain  | Increase [[Hex]]'s [[Target Number]] by +1                                                                                                  | X1              |
|  Heavy Mud   | Hindrance on Athletics Rolls                                                                                                                | X2              |

### Set Piece

Every [[Hex]] should have at least one [[Set Piece]] of interest to the [[Character]]s. It could be the location of a [[Conflict]], a landmark, or other point of interest. It should have at least one [[Scene Feature]] and Encounters should take place here.

# Delving

Any location can be delved as long as it has the following components:

- Entrances
- Barriers that enclose discreet areas. 
- Enclosed sections that form an individual Set Piece.

They can be a building, cave system, fortress, thick hedgerow maze, or other location that meets the three requirements.

## Five Room Dungeon

Five Room Dungeons are the locations of small adventures that can take a single adventuring day. They assume a [[Day's Rest]] and some amount of travel occurred before delving them. 
The Rooms don't necessarily have to be a Room, they are abstractions of [[Set Piece]]s. They don't have to be in linear order. Each [[Set Piece]] should progress the plot, but may be laid out so the [[Player]]s have to choose between them or can choose the order they encounter them. 

### Set Piece 1 - Entrance

The dungeon needs to have some reason it has not been delved before. This could be for multiple reasons:

#### There is a Guardian Creature

A creature or group of creatures are preventing access. They could have made camp outside the entrance, they may have fortified the entrance, or they are otherwise tied to the entrance. Generally they have a [[Quarrelsome]] [[Demeanor]] towards the Characters, allowing them to approach how they want to.

#### The Entrance is Trapped


An entrance trap should be more than just a randomly triggerable hazard, it should require the [[Character]]s to purposefully take a risk to pass it. Such risks should include a [[Proactive Roll]] to attempt to bypass the trap, with the trap's effects being dependent on the [[Roll]]'s outcome.

#### The Entrance is Locked Behind a Challenge


A puzzle or other challenge that may or may not include a risk can be preventing the [[Character]]s from entering.

#### The Entrance Needs to be Found

In this case the [[Character]]'s Preparations come into play. If they [[Gather Information|Gathered Information]] on the dungeon they should have hints on where to find its entrance. This can be resolved with the [[Player]]s solving the puzzle of the entrance's location, or making a [[Complex Roll]] to find it. However, a [[Complex Roll]] should only be used if [[Complication]]s or [[Failure]]s will heighten the stakes. 

#### There is a Choice of Entrances

There are multiple entrances that using one changes the order of [[Set Piece]]s or otherwise change their nature.

### Set Piece 2 - Challenge

Once they are within the Dungeon they should face a challenge. This challenge should be of a different type than the previous room.

#### A Puzzle

This should involve Player's problem solving with their wits and their equipment. Success should require action by multiple Characters, but should not be bypass-able with a Complex Roll. If the Players insist on Rolling, each Roll should stand alone with its own consequences. 

Game Masters should avoid puzzles with a single solution and be open for inventive or clever solutions that can bypass the puzzle or meet its requirements.

#### A Roleplay Encounter

The Characters encounter an [[Antagonist]] of [[Indifferent]] or [[Quarrelsome]] to them that they can engage with multiple ways. These creatures should be able to be negotiated with, or bypassed with clever use of items and [[Proactive Roll]]s.

#### A Risk

The room should have a triggerable hazard that either is triggered by the [[Character]]s' behavior or in response to a [[Proactive Roll]] as a consequence.

### Set Piece 3 - New Conflict

This room should build tension making the stakes higher or more immediate.

#### Commit to the Climax

The Characters must pass a hazard that will prevent them from escaping if overwhelmed by the Climax.

#### Twist

The [[Character]]s are presented with a [[Challenge]] that reframes the story, changing the [[Character]]'s relationship to the dungeon. Some examples are:

- New information about the climax [[Antagonist]]'s motivations or capabilities.
- Discovering the [[Character]]s are operating under false pretenses.
- Revelation about the true nature of the [[Hook]].

#### Sacrifice

The [[Character]]s must make some sort of sacrifice to proceed. This could mean they engage in combat and expend resources before the climax, or encounter a puzzle that reduces their resources in some way.

### Set Piece 4 - Climax

The [[Character]]s enter a conflict that defines the outcome of the adventure. This could be an [[Unique Antagonist]] and their allies who will attack the [[Character]]s, is defending an item, or will prevent the interruption of an action. If the [[Antagonist]] is not immediately [[Hostile]] to the [[Character]]s, they should have [[Goal]]s that are so in conflict with the [[Player]]'s goals that they are mutually exclusive.

### Set Piece 5 - Resolution

Once the Climax has been resolved the [[Character]]s are presented with its aftermath, any rewards for overcoming the climax, and a [[Hook]] for 1 to 3 future adventures. Any further questions that the [[Player]]s may have had about the [[Antagonist]] should be answered, unless they are part of the next adventure. If the [[Character]]s are seeking an item or information, they should find it or find where it actually is (do this rarely as it can be discouraging to [[Player]]s if overused or unexpected). New information about the setting, ongoing [[Antagonist]]s, or the overall plot should be gained.

## Jaquaysed Dungeon

A Jaquaysed Dungeon assumes that the [[Character]]s will take multiple expeditions to delve it, or require taking a [[Day's Rest]] within the Dungeon. They have multiple types of challenges similar to those available in the Five Room Dungeon spread throughout them and the [[Character]]s are not expected to encounter them all. As well, encounters within the Jaquaysed Dungeon should be intentional, with few if any [[Random Encounter]]s. Rather the encounters should be based on the inhabitant's habits and how they react to the Character's intrusion. Expanding on the Five Room Dungeon they can feature:

- **Multiple Entrances** Depending on the entrance used will affect the rest of the dungeon, from the inhabitants' [[Demeanor]]s, to what rooms are accessible, to what is bypassed.
- **Loops** Series of rooms loop back and connect to previous rooms.
- **Dead Ends** Rooms that lead to no other room.
- **Multiple Levels** There are multiple groups of rooms that are connected to both adjacent levels or that bypass adjacent levels. 
- **Secret Paths** There are connections between rooms and levels that need to be discovered either through searching or solving puzzles.
- **Settled Inhabitants** [[Antagonist]]s of any [[Demeanor]] that make their home within the dungeon with varying strengths of [[Motivation]] and [[Goal]]s. Different communities should have conflicting [[Goal]]s with other communities that lead to Conflicts without the [[Character]]s' intervention.
- **Nested Dungeons** Five Room Dungeons or otherwise enclosed sections of dungeon that act as their own mini dungeon with their own thematically consistent features.
- **Keys and Locks** While not always literally keys and locks, they are blocked off sections of dungeons that require [[Character]]s to find an Item, perform an action, or solve a puzzle elsewhere in the Dungeon. 
- **Landmarks** Unique [[Set Pieces]] that the [[Player]]s can use as a landmark to orient themselves with.

> ###### Mapping
> 
> Either the [[Game Master ]]can reveal the map as the characters move from [[Set Piece]] to [[Set Piece]], or they can reveal each individual [[Set Piece]] and put it on the Players to keep track of where they are on the map depending on what the [[Player]]s find most enjoyable. Revealing the map room by room can give hints on the layout of the Dungeon, whereas having the [[Player]]s keep track of the map predicting the dungeon layout can challenge their wits.

## Megadungeon

Megadungeons require multi day expeditions or [[Character]]s only explore a small section at a time. They have all the features of a Jaquaysed Dungeon drastically expanded. The [[Character]]s are expected to be able to support themselves long term without leaving the dungeon to resupply. While they may be able to secure territory or negotiate for protection to take [[Comfortable Rest]]s within the dungeon, they will have to leave to take [[Downtime]].

Their inhabitants are even more populous and factional than a Jaquaysed Dungeon. Different communities should not only have their own goals and motivations, there should be factions that cross communities with conflicting goals and motivations. Conflicts between factions within communities can create more complex and deeper plots for the [[Character]]s to explore.

Inhabitants will not only be encountered within their sections of the dungeon, but can randomly be encountered going about their business throughout the Megadungeon. They should react to the [[Character]]s as their [[Demeanor]] and [[Goal]]s dictate allowing for both beneficial and confrontational encounters.

While within the Megadungeon the [[Character]]s should have discreet goals that act as [[Adventure]]s, and achieving them leads to [[07. Advancement|Advancement]].

> ###### Mapping
> 
> Player mapping of the dungeon is highly encouraged with the Game Master seeding the dungeon with limited maps that the [[Player]]s can use to orient themselves or find secrets. When preparing for entering a Megadungeon, facts learned from [[Gather Information|Gathering Information]] can include limited maps of the Megadungeon.

# Campaigns

Multiple [[Adventure]]s tied together form a Campaign. As they are completed the
overall story of the Campaign emerges. As a [[Game Master]] you are in control of what opportunities the [[Player]]s have for adventure and the pace that those opportunities occur.

# [[Rewards]]

While individual [[Adventure]]s can distribute Rewards unequally among [[Character]]s, by the end of a [[Tier]] the [[Game Master]] should have Rewards for a specific [[Character]] spread somewhat equally across all [[Character]]s.

## [[Milestone]]s

Every [[Character]] has a [[Tier]] that informs the [[Challenge]]s and adventure stakes the [[Game Master]] presents. Depending on how many [[Conflict]]s the [[Character]]s overcome over the [[Adventure]] should be reflected in the Milestones they earn.

## [[Coin]]

Should be limited to the amount of coin easily carried unless the [[Game Master]] wants to challenge the [[Character]]s with risking discarding resources necessary to return to a [[Settlement]] for added Coin. 

## [[Empowered Item]]s

The [[Game Master]] should reward a mix of Empowered Items that are generally useful and are ideal for a specific [[Character]]. The rewards should be thematically appropriate for the [[Adventure]] and its [[Antagonist]]s. Over the course of many [[Adventure]]s each [[Character]] should have at least one item tailored for them as a reward. Generally the [[Character]]s should receive Empowered Items of the same [[Tier]]. If the [[Game Master]] wants to grant an [[Empowered Item]] that's usefulness will span [[Tier]]s they should grant a [[Legacy Item]].

## [[Enhanced Material]]s

If a [[Character]] uses a [[Kit]] or if they plan on crafting [[Empowered Item]]s Enhanced Materials are a welcome reward.

## [[Primer]]

Primers should be thematically tied to the [[Adventure]] or [[Antagonist]] and be on a topic that the [[Player]] has expressed interest in or already begun [[Research]]ing. A Primer's subject should be broad and tied to a theme to allow the Character to potentially use it on multiple [[Research]] topics.

## [[Downtime]]

Players can use Research, Training, and Scribing to add [[Feature]]s to their [[Character]] in exchange for [[Downtime]] spent and [[Lifestyle]] Costs. Every [[Adventure]] should award an amount of [[Downtime]] that occurs between it and the next [[Adventure]]. [[Player]]s can extend the [[Downtime]] but the available adventures progress as they do, changing their nature and possibly causing consequences from avoiding them. 

## [[Title]]s

Tiles are ephemeral awards that act as an Expertise in a specific situation. These should be thematically tied to the adventure and they don't have to be permanent. Players can use a Title to gain an [[Edge]] on a related [[Roll]] 3 times before losing it. Or the [[Character]]'s actions or behavior can lose them a title. Negative titles are possible and may give a -2 bonus to specific situations. And some titles may have positive and negative bonuses tied to different situations.

_Examples._

- **Baron's Favor** [[Expertise]] when negotiating with the Baron for rewards.
- **Oathbreaker** -2 to [[Social]] [[Roll]]s where the [[Character]] makes an oath or a deal that they will do something for an [[Antagonist]].
- **Unhinged Reputation** [[Expertise]] when frightening foes. -2 on Social rolls where the [[Character]] is trying to get an [[Antagonist]] to trust them. 

## Treasure

If the [[Game Master]] wants the [[Character]]s to have more [[Coin]] than would be easily carried, they can include [[Treasure]]s that are thematically consistent with the Climax's [[Antagonist]]. As well this can lead to a challenge of risking discarding resources necessary to return to a [[Settlement]] in exchange for a larger reward. Every [[Treasure]] has a value and takes up 1 or more [[Slot]]s.
