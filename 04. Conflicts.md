Any time the [[Character]]s are faced with a challenge, they are in [[Conflict]]. It could be with the [[Environment]], with a puzzle, or with [[Antagonist]]s. 

# [[Antagonist]]s

Antagonists are not necessarily foes of the [[Character]]s, but they do have incentives and desires that put them at cross purposes. They can want to keep what the [[Character]]s desire, need the [[Character]]s to do a task for them, or want to stop what the [[Character]]s are attempting. They could be the merchant who wants [[Coin]] for their goods, a guard wanting to keep the [[Character]]s out of a castle, or a madman attempting to destroy the world. All that matters is that they may act in conflict with what the [[Character]]s want.

## [[Themes]]

Adversaries should have at least one Theme that gives guidance on what [[Role]] and [[Feature]]s they have in the same way an [[Archetype]] informs a [[Character]]. Depending on the Antagonist they should have two or more themes that combine or contradict to create a more interesting individuals.

## [[Motivation]]s

Antagonists need a Motivation that comes in conflict with the [[Player]]'s desires. The strength of their motivations determine what they are willing to risk in their pursuits that are contrary to the [[Character]]s desires.

> ###### Morale
> 
> The Antagonist's Motivations should include the strength of their motivation. If the cost of their goal becomes higher then the Antagonist will break and either attempt to deescalate, make a deal, or flee. These are not numerical values, but statements of how much an Antagonist will risk and what will make them break.

## [[Goal]]s

In every Conflict, the Antagonist has a Goal they wish to achieve. That Goal should be incompatible with the [[Player]]'s goals for the Conflict. It may be as simple as they want money for the item the [[Character]]s want. Or as vast as a ritual they want to complete that will end the world and birth a new one.

Depending on the strength of their [[Motivation]], Adversaries will be willing to negotiate, risk harm, or fight to the death to achieve their goal.

> ###### [[Insight Roll]]
> 
> When talking to an Antagonist, [[Character]]s can make a [[Wits]] + [[Social]] [[Roll]] against the Antagonist's [[Mind]] [[Defense]]. On a [[Success]] they learn either their [[Motivation]] or [[Goal]]. As a [[Perk]] they learn both, and as a [[Complication]] they learn both but the [[Motivation]] or [[Goal]] is inaccurate. On a [[Failure]] they learn nothing. This should be paired with the [[Player]]s describing how they are getting the information or roleplaying as their [[Character]]s in conversation with the Antagonist. 

## [[Demeanor]]

How the Antagonist relates to the [[Character]]s is their Demeanor. Through interactions they may change the Demeanor up and down the ranks of the following scale.

- **[[Allied]]** They will risk themselves for the Characters and potentially set aside their goals in the Character's favor if their motivations align. They have a -4 to Mind defense against [[Character]] Social Rolls.
- **[[Supportive]]** They will offer support that doesn’t put them at risk or run counter to their Goals. They have a -2 to their Mind defense against [[Character]] Social Rolls.
- **[[Indifferent]]** They will not go out of their way to support or hinder the [[Character]]s.
- **[[Quarrelsome]]** They will do what they can to hinder the [[Character]]s without putting themselves at risk. They have a +3 on their Mind defense against [[Character]]'s Social Rolls to convince them to help the [[Character]]s.
- **[[Hostile]]** They are willing to put their lives on the line to hurt the [[Character]]s. They have a +6 on their Mind Defense against [[Character]]'s Social Rolls to convince them to help.

> ###### [[Lifestyle]] Effect
> 
> Depending on the [[Lifestyle]] of the last [[Day's Rest]] or [[Downtime]], the [[Character]]s may have a [[Hindrance]] when dealing with people of different[[ Lifestyle]]s.
> 
> -  **Poor** [[Social]] [[Roll]]s with Noble individuals have a [[Hindrance]].
> - **Comfortable** There are no effects either way.
> - **Wealthy** [[Social]] [[Roll]]s against a Poor individual has an [[Edge]].
> - **Noble** [[Social]] [[Roll]]s against a Poor or Comfortable individual has an [[Edge]].

## [[Role]]s

If a Conflict devolves into combat, Antagonists will take on a Role that -- combined with their Themes --  guides what [[Stat]]s and [[Feature]]s they have available to them. They inform where the Antagonist is most effective in combat, what their strengths are, and their weaknesses.

- **[[Ambusher]]** Uses stealth and being hidden to gain advantage.
- **[[Artillery]]** Stays to the edge of the battlefield firing in with ranged attacks.
- **[[Bruiser]]** Holds their own, but work best with followers and a leader.
- **[[Horde]]** Horde creatures are designed to work in coordination with other horde creatures but are weak on their own.
- **[[Leader]]** Leads groups of Antagonists. They enhance and amplify the abilities of their followers commanding them to greater effectiveness.
- **[[Skirmisher]]** Mobile and versatile the Skirmisher moves through the battlefield supporting their allies with both melee and ranged attacks.
- **[[Solo]]** Can stand on their own against the [[Character]]s with minimal support. They often can take multiple [[Action]]s or [[Response]]s.
- **[[Follower]]** Filling the ranks of the Antagonists they work best when commanded by a [[Leader]], [[Solo]], or [[Bruiser]].

### [[Unique Antagonist]]s

[[Leader]]s, [[Solo]]s, and other one-off Antagonists can be Unique Antagonists. They are more developed than other Antagonists, having multiple often contradicting themes. Their [[Motivation]]s and [[Goal]]s should be more developed and specific. They tend to have more [[Feature]]s available to them with higher [[Target Number]]s that can be more diverse thematically and more complex mechanically. If they are encountered within an environment that they have control of, they can have additional [[Lair Feature]]s that give them advantage in a conflict. Luring the Antagonist out of their lair should be an option for the [[Player]]s to have their [[Character]]s attempt.

> ###### [[Lair Feature]]s
> 
> Lair Features are [[Scene Feature]]s that are triggered by a [[Unique Antagonist]] in their lair during the [[Scene Feature]]'s initiative.


## [[Challenge Rating]] and [[Tier]]

Every Antagonist has a [[Tier]] to inform the [[Game Master]] what [[Tier]] of play they should be introduced in. They also have a Challenge Rating that give an idea of the relative challenge they are to the [[Character]]s. To determine the Challenge Rating Budget for an [[Set Piece]], add each [[Character]]'s [[Tier]] together including any [[Pet]]s to determine the [[Party Strength]]. Then multiply the [[Party Strength ]]by the [[Set Piece]]'s Difficulty's Multiplier to get the [[Challenge Rating Budget]]. Then add Antagonists until the is close -- but not over -- the [[Challenge Rating Budget]]. If there are more non-[[Minion]] [[Antagonist]]s than the [[Party Strength]] divide the count of non-[[Minion]] [[Antagonist]]s by the [[Party Strength]] then multiply that by the current [[Challenge Rating]] tally to get the adjusted total challenge rating. If it goes over the desired [[Challenge Rating Budget]], adjust the amount of [[Antagonist]]s until it is below again.

| [[Set Piece]] | Difficulty <br>Multiplier | [[Milestone]]s |
| :------------ | :-----------------------: | :------------: |
| **Easy**      |            X1             |  1 X [[Tier]]  |
| **Moderate**  |           X1.5            |  2 X [[Tier]]  |
| **Hard**      |            X2             |  3 X [[Tier]]  |
| **Deadly**    |            X4             |  4 X [[Tier]]  |

> ###### [[Minion]]s
> 
> If the [[Tier]] of an [[Antagonist]] is 2 or more [[Tier]]s under the [[Character]]s they become Minions. Minions do not get [[Response]]s, they cannot [[Exposed|Expose]] a [[Character]] and if an attack surpasses the Minion's [[Soak]] the Minion immediately dies. 

Overcoming an [[Set Piece]] awards an amount of [[Milestone]]s based on the [[Set Piece]]'s difficulty which are used to determine [[Character]] [[07. Advancement|Advancement]]. 

## [[Feature]]s

Antagonists have one or more [[Feature]]s that either [[Exposed|Expose]], [[Exploit]], or control the battlefield. The more [[Feature]]s they have, the higher their [[Challenge Rating]]. Their [[Role]] should inform the types of [[Feature]]s they have and their [[Themes]] inform the effects of the [[Feature]]s.

### [[Target Number]]

It is advised that all an [[Antagonist]]'s [[Feature]]s should have the same [[Target Number]] as long as they fit the Antagonist's [[Themes]]. Those that don't should have a -2 to the [[Feature]]'s [[Target Number]] and should not effect the [[Challenge Rating]] as much.

##  [[Defense]]s

[[Antagonist]]s have the four [[Defense]]s: [[Body]], [[Mind]], [[React]], and [[Deflect]] that begin at 6. Increasing or decreasing the [[Defense]]s changes the [[Challenge Rating]]. They have a [[Soak]] of 0 and an [[HP]] of 6. Increasing them should also increase the [[Challenge Rating]].

# [[Challenge]]s

Challenges are situations that the [[Character]]s seek to overcome in a [[Set Piece]]. Generally they are puzzles for the [[Player]]s to solve or obstacles [[Character]]s to overcome. Because they don't tend to have active [[Antagonist]]s the [[Player]]s use clever problem-solving toor [[Proactive Roll]]s to overcome them. This doesn't mean that [[Antagonist]]s cannot arrive later in response to the Character's attempting to overcome the Challenges. Based on the difficulty of the Challenge's difficulty the [[Set Piece]] should award [[Milestone]]s.

# Combat

Reducing an Antagonist's [[Demeanor]] to [[Hostile]] or a [[Character]] resorts to violence will trigger Combat. 

## [[Round]]s

Over the course of a Round, the [[Character]]s act on their [[Turn]] in any order, the [[Antagonist]]s act on their [[Turn]] in any order, then [[Scene Feature]]s trigger. 

## [[Initiative]]

To determine who goes in the first [[Round]] the [[Player]]s roll their [[Character]]s [[React]] against their enemy's highest [[React]]. If a [[Character]] resorts to violence first, they automatically succeed. And if the [[Character]]s are laying in ambush they get an [[Edge]] on their [[Roll]]. 

## [[Turn]]s

During the party's Turn the [[Character]]s can act in any order using their [[Action]], [[Maneuver]], and [[Interaction]] in any order. But they need to use all three before the next [[Character]] can act. If a [[Maneuver]] includes movement, they can use that movement at any time during their Turn.

> ###### [[Response]]s
> 
>  If another [[Character]] uses a [[Response]] they can act in the middle of another [[Character]]'s turn if the [[Trigger]] for the [[Response]] are met.

## Act Economy

In combat both [[Character]]s and [[Antagonist]]s have access to the same act types every Turn.

### [[Action]]s

Features that require Actions take the lion’s share of a [[Turn]]. They do the most, hit the hardest, or have the most spectacular effects. Every [[Character]] has certain access to Actions regardless of [[Archetype]].

- **[[Assist]]** Without a [[Roll]], while [[Threatened|Threaten]]ing the same foe, a [[Character]] can help an ally's [[Roll]] as long as they are trained in the same [[Skill]] or have an applicable [[Expertise]]. The ally gains an [[Edge]] on that [[Roll]].
- **[[Dodge]]** Without a [[Roll]], a [[Character]] has an [[Edge]] on all [[React]] and [[Deflect]] rolls until the beginning of their next turn.
- **[[Grab]]** The creature makes a [[Strength]] + [[Athletics]] [[Conflict Roll]] against the [[Target]]'s [[React]] to give the target creature the [[Grabbed]] condition. While grabbing a target the grabbing creature can move half their movement taking the grabbed creature along with them.
- **[[Hide]]** A [[Character]] can attempt to become hidden by making a [[Wits]] + [[Stealth]] check while in [[Cover]] from the [[Mind]] defense of [[Antagonist]]s that can see them. With a [[Success]], the observers lose track of the [[Character]]’s position in the chaos of battle and the [[Character]] is [[Hidden]]. 
- **[[Shove]]** A [[Character]] can shove a [[Target]] if they are [[Adjacent]] or [[Share a Space|Sharing a Space]] by making a [[Strength]] + [[Athletics]] [[Roll]] against the [[Target]]'s [[Body]]. On a [[Success]] they push the [[Target]] their [[Strength]] in [[Space]]s. As a [[Perk]] they are pushed an additional 2 [[Space]]s or knocked [[Prone]].
- **Weapon Attack** If the [[Character]] is wielding a [[Weapon]] they can make the appropriate [[Weapon Attack Roll]].

### [[Maneuver]]s

Maneuvers are lesser acts that a [[Character]] can take on their turn. They do not rise to the level of an [[Action]], but if the [[Character]] wants to use a second [[Maneuver]] during their turn they can use their [[Action]] instead. Maneuvers can include movement, if so they use the [[Character]]'s [[Stride]] unless otherwise indicated.

- **Command a Pet or Mount** As a Maneuver a character can command a pet or mount to move, [[Hide]], or [[Dodge]]. If the pet or mount is battle-trained it may also be commanded to Attack or Assist. Without a command a pet or mount will attempt to preserve its life by [[Dodge|Dodging]] and staying in place if not threatened, or moving out of combat if they are. If commanded, they will either stay put and Dodge or move where commanded while Evading.
- **Offhand Attack** A weapon with the Fine Tag or Shield can be used to make an Offhand Attack with the appropriate [[Weapon Attack Roll]].
- **Search the Battlefield** A creature makes a [[Wits]] + [[Senses]] [[Roll]] to reveal hidden enemies, [[Scene Feature]]s they can take advantage of, or traps. If a [[Scene Feature]] is revealed that [[Character]] -- and any others they can alert -- is granted an [[Edge]] on defending against them.
- **Move** If the character has a movement type they can move up to that many [[Space]]s
- **Stand** If the [[Character]] is [[Prone]], they can stand.

### [[Interaction]]s

Interactions are saying a few words, interacting with an object, drawing a weapon or item at hand, dropping an item being held, handing off an item to another willing creature, or other simple activity that doesn't require attention while distracted by combat. A creature can only make one interaction in their turn. To used more they need to use a [[Maneuver]] or [[Action]] in its place.

### [[Response]]s

As an immediate reaction to a specific [[Trigger]] another [[Creature]] performs during any turn. If it has a trigger the Response interrupts the triggering [[Action]] and can modify it or cause it to fail. A [[Creature]] can take one Response each round unless otherwise specified.

## Positioning

Because positioning is strategically important it is abstracted into [[Space]]s. The [[Space]] is not only the area the creature takes up, but also the area needed to act without interference. 

### [[Battlemap]]s

The system is designed with a square grid in mind to represent the battlefield. Each [[Space]] abstracts 5ft by 5ft horizontally and 10ft tall or 2m by 2m and 3m tall at the [[Game Master]]’s preference.

> ###### [[Share a Space|Sharing a Space]]
> 
> Two [[Creature]]s can share the same space. While they are in the same space both [[Creature]]s have a [[Hindrance]] on [[Athletics]], [[Enigmas]], and [[Weapons]] rolls. As well, both creatures have a [[Hindrance]] on [[React]] and [[Deflect]] [[Defense]]s. Both creatures are [[Exposed]].

#### [[Token]]s

Creatures should have a Token to indicates their position on the [[Battlemap]] or [[Chasemap]]. 

- **[[Tiny]]** Tiny creatures range form six inches to two feet tall. They can [[Share a Space]] with any other creature without a [[Hindrance]]. Creatures can [[Move Through]] without it being [[Difficult Terrain]]. They only the space they are in is considered [[Adjacent]].
- **[[Small]]** Ranging from two to four feet tall, small creatures take up an entire [[Space]]. They can [[Move Through]] an occupied space without it being [[Difficult Terrain]].
- **[[Medium]]** Ranging from four to seven feet tall, medium creatures take up an entire [[Space]].
- **[[Large]]** Ranging from seven feet tall to sixteen feet tall or cannot fit within a [[Space]], Large creatures take up 2 by 2 [[Space]]s. 
- **[[Huge]]** Larger than sixteen feet tall, Huge creatures take up 4 by 4 spaces. They typically have [[Reach]] 2 for their [[Weapon Attack]]s unless otherwise indicated.
- **[[Colossal]]** Colossal creatures are larger than a Battlemap and rarely do more than protrude onto it. Their individual [[Feature]]s will describe the area they [[Threatened|Threaten]] ad if they have an associated [[Token]].

> ###### [[Adjacent]]
> 
> Adjacent [[Creature]]s are those who's Tokens are adjacent to each other either horizontally, vertically, or diagonally.

## Moving

[[Maneuver]]s indicate if they can include movement. If they do, the Creature can move across Spaces with their listed movement in its type as part of the [[Maneuver]]. Moving diagonally costs 1.5 [[Space]]s per [[Space]] moved and the total move is rounded down.

- **[[Burrow]] #** Burrowing creatures can move through solid earth in any direction.
- **[[Climb]] #** Climbing creatures can move up steep or vertical surfaces without have to make an [[Athletics]] [[Proactive Roll]].
- **[[Fly]] #** A flying creature can move in any direction in empty space. They must move at least half of the Fly distance during their [[Turn]] or they are knocked [[Prone]] begin to fall.
- **[[Hover]] #** Hovering creatures can move in any direction in empty space during their [[Turn]]. They are immune to being made [[Prone]].
- **[[Stride]] #** Striding creatures cam move the listed distance against clear level ground. Steep uphill climbs or [[Difficult Terrain]] take twice the amount of distance to cross a single space.
- **[[Swim]] #** Swimming creatures can move in any direction the current allows. Moving along with the current adds the current speed to the distance the creature can travel. Moving against the current subtracts the current speed from the listed distance. While swimming are immune to being made [[Prone]].

> ###### [[Move Through]]
> 
> A [[Creature]] can move through another [[Creature]]'s space and it is considered [[Difficult Terrain]]. If a [[Creature]] ends their turn within another [[Creature]]'s space they are [[Share a Space|Sharing a Space]]. [[Response]]s by the [[Creature]] who’s space is being moved through have an [[Edge]] against the moving [[Creature]]. If the other [[Creature]] is [[Hostile]], as a [[Response]] they can make a [[Weapon Attack]] against the moving [[Creature]].

### Forced Movement

A [[Feature]] can force a [[Creature]] to move. When moving through [[Hazardous Terrain]], the moved creature is effected as if they moved themself.

- **[[Fling]] #** Move the [[Target]] in a straight line in an direction the listed amount of [[Space]]s.
- **[[Pull]] #** Move the [[Target]] towards the [[Feature]]'s [[Source]] in a straight line the listed amount of [[Space]]s.
- **[[Push]] #** Move the [[Target]] way from the [[Feature]]'s [[Source]] in a straight line the listed amount of [[Space]]s.

> ###### Collisions
> 
> If a [[Creature]] is forced to collide with another creature or object, they take 1d6 for every 2 [[Space]]s they are prevented moving. If the [[Target]] collides with another [[Creature]] they both take half the [[Damage]].

## Targeting

A Feature can target a [[Creature]] or an area of Spaces.

> ###### [[Threatened|Threaten]]
> 
> Targets that are [[Adjacent]] to, or within the [[Reach]] of the [[Creature]] are are [[Threatened]].

### [[Line of Effect]]

For a [[Feature]] to effect a [[Target]], spaces need to be unobstructed emanating from the origin. [[Cover]] obstructs emanations even if they are transparent.

### Targeting Areas

Area effects have an [[Origin Point]] that is in the center of a [[Space]]. Otherwise they [[Target]] a specific [[Creature]] or [[Scene Feature]].

> ###### [[Source]]
> 
> The Source is the [[Creature]] using the [[Feature]].

- **[[Adjacent]]** The effect can target a creature who's space is adjacent to the [[Source]]'s [[Space]]. All melee [[Weapon]]s can target an Adjacent foe.
- **[[Aura]] #** The effect extends in all directions from the target the listed [[Space]]s. Aura 0 only effects creatures in the [[Origin Point]].
- **[[Circle]] #** The radius of a Circle extends around the [[Origin Point]] extending the [[Space]]s listed. Circle 0 only covers the [[Origin Point]].
- **[[Cube]] #** The effect fills an area where the listed [[Space]]s defines each side of the area encompassed in 3d space starting from the [[Origin Point]]. 
- **[[Fan]] #** The area projects outward in a ninety degree fan the listed spaces who's [[Origin Point]] is  adjacent to the [[Source]]'s [[Space]].
- **[[Line]] #** The area projects forward in a line from the center of the [[Source]]'s [[Space]] to a [[Space]] within the noted distance. Every [[Space]] the line crosses is within the effect.
- **[[Range]] #** Either targets a single creature within the listed Spaces from the [[Source]], or the [[Origin Point]] of an area is within the listed [[Space]]s from the [[Source]].
- **[[Reach]] #** [[Threatened|Threatens]] creatures within the listed [[Space]]s of the [[Source]]'s [[Space]].
- **[[Sphere]] #** The area extends around the origin like a Circle but in all directions. Sphere 0 covers the [[Origin Point]].
- **[[Touch]]** The [[Character]] can reach all targets they [[Threatened|Threaten]] with their [[Natural]] [[Weapon]] or are in an [[Adjacent]] space.
- **[[Wall]] #** The area is 1 [[Space]] wide, 2 [[Space]]s tall, and listed [[Space]]s long unless otherwise stated in the description.

> ###### [[Threatened]]
> 
> Targets that are [[Adjacent]] to, or within the [[Reach]] of the [[Creature]] are are Threatened.

## Taking [[Damage]]

When a [[Feature]] deals [[Damage]] subtract from it any Temporary HP the [[Target]] might have. Then subtract the [[Target]]'s [[Soak]] from the remaining [[Damage]], reducing it no lower than 1. Then subtract the remainder from the [[Target]]'s [[HP]]. The [[HP]] can't go lower than 0.

## Ongoing Effects

When a [[Feature]] with a duration that targets a [[Defense]] of a [[Character]], they make a [[Defense Roll]] against the [[Target Number]] at the beginning of their [[Turn]]. If they [[Success|Succeed]] the [[Feature]]'s effect ends.

When an [[Antagonist]] is targeted by an ongoing effect, record the [[Feature]]'s [[Conflict Roll]] and note it as the as the [[Diminishing TN]]. At the beginning of the Antagonist's turn, reduce the [[Diminishing TN]] by 1 until it drops below the Antagonist's [[Defense]].

## [[Condition]]s

Beyond taking [[Damage]], a [[Feature]] may confer a Condition to its [[Target]]s. Conditions are temporary effects that change a [[Creature]]'s capabilities. Most can only be applied once and repeated applications do nothing, with noted exceptions. The source of the condition will have rules on how it ends. 

- **[[Blinded]]** The [[Creature]] cannot see and as such has a [[Hindrance]] on [[Deflect]], [[Enigmas]], [[React]], [[Senses]], [[Weapons]] [[Roll]]s and are [[Exposed]]. Other [[Creature]]s have an [[Edge]] on [[Roll]]s that target the Blinded [[Creature]]. 
- **[[Blighted]]** Blight targets a single [[Stat]] of the [[Character]] for a number of levels. All [[Roll]]s that include the Blighted [[Stat]] subtract a D4 from that roll for each level of Blight. If a Character has more blight in one [[Stat]] as 2 plus their [[Fortitude]] [[Stat]] they become [[Incapacitated]]. For every Blight applies to an [[Antagonist]], reduce the [[Target Number]]s of [[Feature]]s and [[Defense]]s by 1.
- **[[Bleeding]]** The creature loses one [[HP]] at the start of their turn until they are healed at least 1 [[HP]]. Bleeding can be applied multiple times, the damage increases by 1 for each Bleeding applied. If the creature is at 0 [[HP]] they no longer have the Bleeding Condition but are dying as normal.
- **[[Burning]]** At the the beginning of the Burning [[Creature]]'s turn [[Turn]] take 1d6 [[Damage]] and are [[Exposed]]. To end Burning condition the creature or an adjacent creature can snuff the flames as a Maneuver or fully be immersed in water.
- **[[Charmed]]** The Charmed [[Creature]] cannot attack the creature that charmed it. The Charmed [[Creature]] has a [[Hindrance]] on rolls that use [[Will]] or their [[Mind]] when interacting with the [[Creature]] that charmed it. Charmed [[Creature]]s are [[Exposed]].
- **[[Deafened]]** The character cannot hear and as such as a [[Hindrance]] on [[React]], [[Senses]], and [[Social]] [[Roll]]s. [[Stealth]] [[Roll]]s have [[Edge]] against deafened [[Creature]]s. Deafened [[Creature]]s are [[Exposed]].
- **[[Exposed]]** [[Exploit]] [[Feature]]s can effect the [[Creature]].
- **[[Frightened]]** The [[Creature]] cannot move towards the source of their fear. Frightened [[Character]]s have a [[Hindrance]] on all [[Roll]]s while they can sense the source of their fear. [[Character]]s that inspire fear have an [[Edge]] on rolls targeting the [[Mind]] defense of [[Frightened]] [[Antagonist]]s. Frightened creatures are [[Exposed]].
- **[[Grabbed]]** The Grabbed creature's movement is reduced to 0 and their [[Weapon Attack Roll]]s have a [[Hindrance]] unless using a [[Weapons]] with the [[Fine]] [[Tag]]. Grabbed creatures are [[Exposed]]. As an [[Action]] the Grabbed [[Creature]] can attempt to escape by comparing their [[Dexterity]] + [[Athletics]] or [[Strength]] + [[Athletics]] to the a new [[Roll]] of the [[Feature]] that grabbed them. If a [[Feature]] would move a [[Grabbed]] [[Creature]] then compare it's [[Roll]] or [[Target Number]] to the [[Roll]] or [[Target Number]] of the [[Feature]] that Grabbed them.
- **[[Incapacitated]]**  The creature cannot make an [[Action]], [[Maneuver]] other than to move, [[Response]], or [[Interaction]]. Incapacitated [[Creature]]s are [[Exposed]].
- **[[Paralyzed]]**  The creature cannot make an [[Action]], [[Maneuver]], [[Response]], [[Interaction]], or speak. They automatically fail [[React]] and [[Deflect]] [[Roll]]s. Attacks against a Paralyzed creature gain a [[Perk]] . Paralyzed creatures are [[Exposed]].
- **[[Petrified]]** Petrified [[Creature]]s and their held items are changed to an inanimate substance. The creature cannot make an [[Action]], make a [[Maneuver]], make a [[Response]], move, or speak. They also have a [[Durability]] of 6, and are immune to poison, disease, and aging. Poisons and diseases are not neutralized, just suspended. Petrified creatures are [[Exposed]].
- **[[Prone]]**  Prone [[Creature]]s can only crawl using two [[Space]]s of movement for every [[Space]] traveled. Standing up spends half their movement speed. The prone creature has a Hindrance on attack rolls. Prone creatures are Exposed to melee attacks.
- **[[Restrained]]**  Restrained [[Creature]]'s movement is reduced to 0. They have a [[Hindrance]] on their [[React]] and [[Deflect]] [[Roll]]. The Restrained [[Character]] has a [[Hindrance]] on all [[Roll]]s, and [[Defense]]s against an [[Antagonist]]'s [[Feature]]s have an [[Edge]]. Restrained [[Creature]]s are [[Exposed]].
- **[[Sickened]]** Sickened [[Character]]s have a [[Hindrance]] on all [[Roll]]s. All [[Roll]]s against a Sickened [[Antagonist]] have an [[Edge]].
- **[[Suffocating]]**  A [[Creature]] can suffocate for a number of rounds equal to their [[Body]]. After that they begin making[[ Stabilization Roll]]s at the beginning of they turn for as long as they are Suffocating. As a [[Perk]] they turn one [[Failure]] into a [[Success]]. Once they [[Failure|Fail]] three total [[Stabilization Roll]]s they die.
- **[[Unconscious]]** The [[Creature]] cannot make an [[Action]], [[Maneuver]], [[Response]], [[Interaction]], or speak. They automatically fail [[React]] and [[Deflect]] [[Roll]]s. Unconscious creatures are [[Exposed]]. They can be awakened by taking [[Damage]] or another [[Creature]] performs a [[Maneuver]] that includes movement to awaken them.

## [[Dying]]

If a [[Character]] drops to 0 [[HP]] or below they fall [[Unconscious]] take a [[Blighted|Blight]] of their choice and begin [[Dying]]. On the [[Character]]'s [[Turn]] they make a [[Stabilization Roll]] based on their [[Fortitude]] against [[Target Number]] 8. If they [[Roll]] three [[Success]]es before three [[Failure]]s the [[Character]] stops Dying. If they take [[Damage]] they gain one [[Failure]], if the [[Damage]] is greater than [[Character]]' the [[Maximum HP]] they gain an additional [[Failure]].

- **Roll a 12 on the D12** Two [[Success]]es.
- **Roll over or equal to the TN** One [[Success]].
- **Roll under the TN** One [[Failure]].
- **Roll a 1 on the D12** Two [[Failure]]s.

If an [[Antagonist]] drops to 0 [[HP]] they fall [[Unconscious]] and begin [[Dying]]. Depending on their [[Role]], they have a set number of rounds before they actually die that they can receive treatment or otherwise recover [[HP]].

- **1 Round** [[Horde]], [[Follower]].
- **2 Rounds** [[Ambusher]], [[Artillery]], [[Skirmisher]]
- **3 Rounds** [[Bruiser]], [[Leader]], [[Solo]]

# [[Chase]]s

Sometimes the conflict cannot stay on a [[Battlemap]] as one or more [[Creature]]s are fleeing and the rest are chasing. With Chases the Game Master needs to track for each participant much Movement they make during their Turn to be applied at the end of the [[Round]]. It is best to use a [[Chasemap]] to visualize all participants in a single line, with the terrain dictating how many [[Token]]s can share a point on the line. 

## [[Chasemap]]

A Chasemap is a grid with a [[Lead]] position in the middle of one edge and at least 15 [[Space]]s deep with a line down the center that describes how far the participants are from the [[Lead]]. Each [[Token]] takes up their normal space along the center [[Lane]]. On either side of the center [[Lane]] are the amount of [[Lane]]s that the chase's environment will allow characters to be the same distance form the [[Lead]].

> ###### Resizing the [[Space]]s
> 
> If the participants are all vehicles or other things that take up more than 1 [[Space]], scale up the Spaces that a single Space on the Chasemap represents [[Space]]s to match. Then divide the movement of the participants and their ranges by that scale to better model the Chasemap.

## [[Lead]]

The pace of the Chase and the order of turns is set by the Lead. This participant stays in the Lead Space on the [[Chasemap]]. The Lead's side goes first in the [[Round]], followed by their opponent's side. The Chase continues until the Lead is forced to stop or all participants on the opposing side [[Fall Behind]].

> ###### [[Fall Behind]]
> 
> Once a participant is moved off the Chasemap they are out of the Chase. That doesn't mean they leave initiative. On their turn the [[Game Master]] should challenge the Player to find a way for their [[Character]] to rejoin the chase on their turn.

Not only does the Lead set the pace, they also choose the path the Chase will take. At the beginning of the [[Round]], the Lead takes their [[Turn]]. The [[Game Master]] should provide two to four choices of path for the Lead to take. Each one should have its own challenges and opportunities. Unless it is [[Screened]] or takes a circuitous path through [[Hazardous Terrain]], for each path the [[Game Master]] should describe the terrain they would be moving through, including any [[Target Number]]s for passing the visible hazards, for the [[Round]].

## Starting Positions

Every participant starts at a set distance from the [[Lead]] based on their separation on the [[Battlemap]]. The Lead can move their full speed before the chase begins setting the initial distances. If a [[Character]] is chasing they can make a [[Wits]] + [[Senses]] [[Conflict Roll]] against the Lead's [[React]] to predict the [[Lead]]'s intention. And if a [[Character]] is initiates the Chase, they become the Lead and make a [[Wits]] + [[Social]] roll to move without warning. On a [[Success]] [[Creature]]s start the Chase at a distance equal to their distance to the [[Lead]] before the Chase began. As a [[Perk]] they can close half their movement  If the [[Lead]] can communicate unobserved to Allies, they all succeed on their [[Roll]]. Otherwise all other participants start at their previous distance + the [[Lead]]'s movement.

## [[Terrain Hazard]]s

When in a chase, Terrain Hazards are things that interfere with the chase. Participants use their Maneuver to make a [[Chase Roll]] against the Terrain's [[Target Number]]. On-Foot they use their [[Dexterity]] and when Mounted they use the [[Mount]]'s [[Dexterity]]. Failing a [[Chase Roll]] within a Terrain Hazard causes a [[Crash]].

> ###### [[Crash]]
> 
> Movement for this turn is reduced to 0. If they were moving more than 6 Spaces this turn, they take 1d6 Damage for each 2 Spaces beyond 6.

_Examples_.

- **Crowds** Non-Participant that are in the way that will stop the Participant and share any damage the Participant takes form the Crash.
- **Buildings** Tight corridors or buildings make for navigational hazards and can lead to Constriction.
- **Debris** Debris on the path that can cause damage to participants and their vehicles with a Target Number to avoid.
- **[[Hazardous Terrain]]** The [[Hazardous Terrain]]'s [[Target Number]] is the Terrain's [[Target Number]].

> ###### Traps
> 
> Any traps set along the path target the [[Lead]] before they can take a turn. If they are already aware of the Trap, they have an [[Edge]] on their [[Defense Roll]] against it. As a [[Perk]] on their [[Defense]] they avoid the Trap completely and are aware of it, gaining an [[Edge]] on [[Juke]] this [[Turn]] to direct a following participant into it.

### Constriction

Sometimes the path constricts, preventing multiple participants from being the same distance from the lead. In those cases, reduce the amount of [[Lane]]s from the center [[Lane]] a participant can occupy.

## Participants

There are many ways to participate in a chase, On-Foot, Mounted, Driving a Vehicle, or be a Passenger.

- **On-Foot** When on foot, [[Chase Roll]]s use the Participant's [[Fortitude]] [[Stat]] and [[Athletics]] [[Skill]] unless otherwise stated.
- **Mounted** When mounted, [[Chase Roll]]s use the [[Mount]]'s [[Fortitude]] [[Stat]] and the Participant's [[Pilot]] [[Skill]] unless otherwise stated. the Participant is considered Mounted if they are directing thinking creatures, no matter how basic.
- **In Vehicle** When driving a [[Vehicle]], [[Chase Roll]]s use the driver's [[Wits]] [[Stat]] and [[Pilot]] [[Skill]] unless otherwise stated.
- **Passenger** Passengers don't make [[Chase Roll]]s. They take their turn after the driver of their vehicle.

> ###### [[Chase Roll]]s
> 
> Chase Rolls have the same scale as a [[Proactive Roll]]. Chases have their own list of [[Chase Complication]]s for the [[Game Master ]]to choose from.
> 
> - **12 on Die and over [[Target Number|TN]]** [[Success]] with a [[Perk]].
> - **Over or equal to [[Target Number|TN]]** [[Success]].
> - **Under [[Target Number|TN]]** [[Success]] with a [[Chase Complication]].
> - **1 on the Die** [[Failure]] and the Character Falls Behind equal to the Lead's movement.

## [[Chase Action]]s

When in a chase participants whether on foot, riding a mount, or piloting a vehicle need to use a [[Maneuver]] that includes movement or else they will [[Fall Behind]]. 

> ###### Passengers
> 
> Each Chase Action causes passengers to have a [[Hindrance]] on [[Proactive Roll|Proactive]] and [[Conflict Roll]]s the turn they are made unless otherwise stated.

- **[[Accelerate]]** As an [[Action]], double the participant's movement this [[Turn]]. Can only be done an amount of turns equals to their [[Fortitude]] in a single [[Chase]] without taking a level of [[Blinded|Blight]] each subsequent time. Passengers do not gain [[Hindrance]] from this.
- **[[Disappear]]** When traveling through terrain that can [[Screened|Screen]] the participant they try and use the terrain to [[Hide]] from other participants. Make a [[Chase Roll ]]against any viewers' [[Mind]] to [[Hide]].
- **[[Elude]]** As an [[Action]] the participant attempts to be harder to hit. On-foot participants use [[Dexterity]] and Mounted participants use the [[Mount]]'s [[Dexterity]] in their [[Chase Roll]]. On a [[Success]] the participant, [[Mount]], [[Vehicle]], and passengers gain an [[Edge]] on [[Deflect]] and [[React]] [[Defense]]s.
- **[[Hold Steady]]** As an [[Action]] and maintain course with minimal disturbance through rough terrain. If passing through [[Difficult Terrain]] make a [[Chase Roll]] against the [[Target Number]] of the terrain to maintain their speed and avoid any potential [[Terrain Hazard]]s. Passengers do not gain a [[Hindrance]] from this.
- **[[Juke]]** As an [[Action]], the pilot attempts to direct a following participant into [[Terrain Hazard]]s they are passing through or adjacent to with a [[Chase Roll]] using Wits against the following participant's [[Mind]].
- **[[Swap]]** As an [[Action]] the participant attempts to get behind a following participant by making a [[Chase Roll]] using [[Wits]] against the target's [[Mind]].
- **Use Emplaced Weapon** When using an [[Emplaced]] [[Weapon]] is fixed to the vehicle, as an [[Action]] the driver makes an [[Weapon Attack Roll]] with their [[Wits]] + [[Pilot]] to line up their shot against the target's [[React]] or [[Deflect]] depending on the [[Weapon]]'s [[Tag]]s. 

### [[Chase Complication]]s

- **Glancing Blow** The participant hits a hazard but doesn't slow. Takes 1d6 [[Damage]] for every 4 spaces beyond 6 that they ware traveling at.
- **Rattled** The participant and passengers have a [[Hindrance]] on [[Proactive Roll|Proactive]] and [[Conflict Roll]]s  until the end of the participant's next [[Turn]].
- **Slowed** The participant moves down the [[Chasemap]] half their movement. If the [[Lead]] is Slowed, move all participants up half the [[Lead]]'s movement.

### [[Chase Perk]]s

Chase Perks should be chosen by the [[Player]] that rolled them. They can only choose one.
- **Shortcut** The participant moves up the [[Chasemap]] half their movement. If the participant is the [[Lead]], move all other participants down the [[Chasemap]] by half the [[Lead]]'s movement. 
- **Interference** If the participant is ahead of another they can move through Hazardous or Difficult Terrain without rolling, leading Participants that follow into it.
- **Lock On** If the Participant is behind another, they have an Edge on Proactive and Combat Rolls. 

## Adjusting Position

At the end of each [[Round]], subtract from each participant's Movement from the [[Lead]]'s movement. Move them up and down the [[Chasemap]] based on that difference. [[Player]]s can choose to not close on the [[Lead]] even if they have the movement. And they can slow to increase the distance between them and the [[Lead]] at this point. The terrain may limit how many [[Creature]]s can move at the same distance from the [[Lead]].  Moving between lanes does not affect overall movement that turn. And as long as one additional lane is open, a [[Creature]] can always pass another without moving through the same space.

> ###### [[Falling Behind]]
> 
> When a participant leaves the [[Chasemap]] they have fallen behind and are no longer a participant. But that doesn't mean they leave [[Initiative]]. After all participants on their side have gone, the [[Player]] can propose ways for them to get back on the [[Chasemap]] during their turn to work to rejoin the chase.

## Rejoining the Chase

Chases don't happen in straight lines. The wend and weave as the Lead chooses a new path every [[Round]]. This provides opportunities for those who Fall Behind to rejoin the Chase. Every turn they make one [[Roll]] of a [[Complex Roll ]]against the Mind defense of the [[Lead]] of [[Wits]] + [[Social]] to predict where the [[Lead]] is going (if the Character can communicate with allies in the chase gain an Edge), [[Wits]] + [[Knowledge]] to determine a cut off, and a Fortitude + Athletics to push to catch up or [[Dexterity]] + [[Athletics]] to slip around obstacles. On a [[Success]] they rejoin the [[Chasemap]] [[Lead]]'s movement behind the [[Lead]]. As a [[Perk]] they rejoin at their movement adjacent to the [[Lead]]. As a [[Complication]] they rejoin at the edge of the [[Chasemap]]. And on a [[Failure]] they do not catch up and need to make another [[Complex Roll]].
